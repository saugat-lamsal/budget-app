PHASE 7: CONNECTION DAY (plain steps)

Nothing here happens until you say so. This is the first time real money
comes near the app: one of your 10 Plaid connections gets used, and the
hosted books start fresh for your real accounts.

Built before it (branch before-phase-7): the backups' heartbeat (A43), the
Bank screen (rules 1.1 to 1.3), the permission end date (10.13), and the
production switch (off until this day). Tried first on this PC against
Plaid's test bank through the real Plaid login (the rehearsal).


ONCE, BEFORE THE DAY (yours, about 15 minutes; creates nothing)

1. Plaid dashboard (dashboard.plaid.com):
   a. The home page says Trial plan, 0 of 10 Items. If it says "Limited
      Production", or asks you to apply for Production, stop and tell me.
   b. Do not submit a Production application. It is one-way and ends the
      free Trial.
   c. OAuth institutions page: Bank of America is available.
   d. Never use Plaid's own Quickstart or test tools with Production keys:
      every connection made there counts against the 10, even if removed.
2. Your Production secret is under Developers > Keys. Leave it there until
   step 3 of the day. Never paste it in a chat.
3. The rehearsal, on this PC (about 10 minutes): the same Bank screen
   against Plaid's test bank, through Plaid's real login window. Test-bank
   connections do not count against the 10; its books are thrown away when
   it stops.
     npm run rehearsal
   a. In Chrome open http://localhost:5393 and click "Log this browser in".
   b. Bank connection > Connect the bank > Yes, connect. Plaid's window
      opens, saying "Sandbox mode" at the bottom.
   c. "Continue without phone number", pick a bank: "First Platypus Bank",
      log in with Plaid's published test login, user_good / pass_good.
      (Or "Platypus OAuth Bank", which logs in through the bank's own
      window, the way Bank of America does.) Tick every account.
   d. Back in the app: pick checking, savings, the everyday card and the
      old card (the test bank has only one credit card, so pick any other
      account for the old card), Save, Yes. If it says the history is still
      coming, "Look again" in a minute.
   e. "Log in to the bank again": Plaid's window opens on the same
      connection; close it or go through it, and the accounts stay.
   f. The control page (http://localhost:5393) shows what the books hold:
      connected, the four picked, and items read. Stop it with Ctrl+C.
   Already checked from here (Oct 12): it starts, logs in, "Connect the
   bank" asks first, Plaid's window opens in Sandbox mode, and closing it
   says "Nothing was connected." Steps c to e need you: the login is typed
   into Plaid's window.


ON THE DAY (together, in this order; anything odd, we stop)

1. A new bank key for the new books, on this PC:
     node -e "console.log(require('node:crypto').randomBytes(32).toString('base64'))"
   Keep it where you keep a bank password. (The old one only ever sealed
   the test bank's key.)

2. The switch, a small commit of mine, at your word: fly.toml says
   PLAID_ENV "production", mounts the new volume (budget_real), and the
   backups go to a new folder in the same bucket. test/sandbox-only.test.ts
   changes with it: fly.toml alone may say production. CI runs on it.

3. The secrets: a file .env.fly with two lines,
     PLAID_SECRET=      (the Production secret)
     BANK_TOKEN_KEY=    (from step 1)
   then
     Get-Content .env.fly | fly secrets import --stage --app budget-app-x7q2
   (--stage: nothing restarts yet) and delete .env.fly. PLAID_CLIENT_ID,
   the push keys and the R2 keys stay as they are.

4. The new volume, and the machine moved onto it:
     fly volumes create budget_real --app budget-app-x7q2 --region ewr --size 1 --yes
     fly machine list --app budget-app-x7q2          (note the one machine's id)
     fly machine destroy <id> --app budget-app-x7q2 --force
     fly deploy --ha=false --remote-only
   The old volume (budget_data, the test bank's books) and the old backup
   folder are not touched: they are the way back. Delete them yourself
   later (about $0.15 a month until then).
   The log should say: "starting with empty books", "Budget app: the real
   bank, through Plaid, hosted", and "No bank is connected yet".

5. Checks: fly status (one machine), /health says ok, /health/backups says
   ok. In UptimeRobot add a second monitor: HTTP(s),
   https://budget-app-x7q2.fly.dev/health/backups, every 5 minutes, alert
   your email and the app. Try it as in Phase 6 (a wrong address, then put
   it back).

6. Log in on the PC (Chrome): open the app, "Use a one-time code",
     fly ssh console -C "node /app/serve/login-code.mjs"
   and make a passkey (Windows Hello).

7. Connect, on the PC: Settings > Bank card > Bank connection > Connect the
   bank. It asks first (1 of the 10). Yes. Plaid asks for a phone number:
   "Continue as guest" is fine. Bank of America's own login opens (through
   Plaid, in a window of its own; "Return to institution" brings it back if
   it is behind). On Bank of America's own screen, tick all four accounts.
   Back in the app: pick which is checking, savings, the everyday card and
   the old card; Save; Yes. If Plaid is still sending the history, "Look
   again" in a minute or two.
   If one account is missing: Log in to the bank again (no new connection)
   and tick it.

8. Setup day, with your real balances (Phase 7, step 2 of the roadmap).

9. On the iPhone: open the app from the home screen, log in with a new
   one-time code, make a passkey, then delete the old passkey for
   budget-app-x7q2.fly.dev in the Passwords app (the new books do not know
   it). Settings > Notifications > Turn on.

10. The restore check on the machine:
      fly ssh console -C "node /app/serve/restore-check.mjs"
    The last line must be: The backup works.


THE WAY BACK (before setup day only)

fly.toml back to sandbox, the old volume and backup folder, the sandbox
secret, and a deploy: the test bank's books come back as they were. They
can no longer read the test bank (its key was sealed with the old bank key,
replaced in step 3), which is fine for made-up data. The Plaid connection
made in step 7 stays used (1 of the 10): connections are never removed.
(Each fly command's flags are checked against flyctl's own help on the day.)

   It builds the new image on Fly (nothing is deployed), rebuilds the real

3. Check: https://budget-app-x7q2.fly.dev/health says ok, /health/backups
   says ok, fly logs shows the server listening and Litestream copying, and
   the restore check on the machine:
     fly ssh console -C "node /app/serve/restore-check.mjs"
