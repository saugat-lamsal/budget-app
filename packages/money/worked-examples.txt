WORKED EXAMPLES

Made-up numbers, worked out step by step, so you can check the money math
by hand. Every amount is in dollars and cents.

Each example is also a test (packages/money/test/worked-examples.test.ts).
The test reads the "Check:" line of every example straight from this file
and compares it with what the app works out. If a number here is wrong, or
the app disagrees with it, the test fails.

Unless an example says otherwise, the settings are the starting ones from
the rulebook: split 50% savings / 30% needs / 20% wants, mom reserve
$1,500.00 a paycheck, capped at $3,000.00.

Rounding: each share is rounded DOWN to the cent. Any cents left over from
rounding go to savings (rule 3.3).


========================================================================
EXAMPLE 1. A normal paycheck (rules 3.2, 3.3)
========================================================================

Before payday: every bucket is at $0.00.

Paycheck                                         2,345.67

Step 1. The mom reserve comes off first.
        Reserve is $0.00, cap is $3,000.00, so there is room.
        To mom reserve                          -1,500.00
        Rest to split                              845.67

Step 2. Split the rest.
        Savings 50% of 845.67 = 422.835  ->  422.83
        Needs   30% of 845.67 = 253.701  ->  253.70
        Wants   20% of 845.67 = 169.134  ->  169.13
        Rounded shares add to 845.66, so 0.01 is left over.
        The leftover 0.01 goes to savings: 422.83 + 0.01 = 422.84

Step 3. Add it back up: 1,500.00 + 422.84 + 253.70 + 169.13 = 2,345.67
        That is the paycheck exactly.

Check: mom reserve 1,500.00 | savings 422.84 | needs 253.70 | wants 169.13

EXAMPLE 2. The mom reserve is close to its cap (rule 3.2)
========================================================================

Before payday: mom reserve $2,600.00. Everything else $0.00.

Paycheck                                         3,000.00

Step 1. The reserve takes $1,500.00 off the top, but only $400.00 fits
        under the $3,000.00 cap (3,000.00 - 2,600.00 = 400.00).
        To mom reserve                             400.00
        Does not fit, goes to savings            1,100.00
        Rest to split (3,000.00 - 1,500.00)      1,500.00

Step 2. Split the rest.
        Savings 50% of 1,500.00 =   750.00
        Needs   30% of 1,500.00 =   450.00
        Wants   20% of 1,500.00 =   300.00
        No rounding needed.

Step 3. Buckets after payday.
        Mom reserve  2,600.00 + 400.00            = 3,000.00 (at the cap)
        Savings      1,100.00 + 750.00            = 1,850.00
        Needs                                         450.00
        Wants                                         300.00
        Paycheck check: 400.00 + 1,850.00 + 450.00 + 300.00 = 3,000.00

Check: mom reserve 3,000.00 | savings 1,850.00 | needs 450.00 | wants 300.00

EXAMPLE 3. A paycheck smaller than the mom reserve (rule 3.2)

Before payday: mom reserve $2,000.00. Everything else $0.00.

Paycheck                                         1,200.00

Step 1. The reserve would take $1,500.00, but the paycheck is only
        $1,200.00, so it can take at most $1,200.00.
        Room under the cap: 3,000.00 - 2,000.00 = 1,000.00.
        To mom reserve                           1,000.00
        Does not fit, goes to savings              200.00
        Rest to split                                0.00

Step 2. Nothing is left to split. Needs and wants get $0.00.

Check: mom reserve 3,000.00 | savings 200.00 | needs 0.00 | wants 0.00


========================================================================
EXAMPLE 4. Payday with money left over and an overspent bucket
           (rules 3.2, 3.3, 3.4, 3.5, 3.8)
========================================================================

Before payday:
        Savings 0.00 | Needs 85.20 | Wants -12.50 (overspent) |
        Mom reserve 3,000.00 (at the cap)
        The savings account at the bank holds 3,000.00.
        Leftover setting: ask each time.

Paycheck                                         2,800.00

Step 1. Leftovers from the period that just ended.
        Needs has 85.20 unspent, so the app asks: roll over or move?
        Wants is at -12.50. It carries over as a negative. Nothing to ask.

Step 2. The reserve is at its cap, so the whole 1,500.00 goes to
        savings instead.
        Rest to split: 2,800.00 - 1,500.00 = 1,300.00
        Savings 50% = 650.00 | Needs 30% = 390.00 | Wants 20% = 260.00

Step 3. Buckets after payday, before you answer.
        Savings  1,500.00 + 650.00 = 2,150.00
        Needs       85.20 + 390.00 =   475.20
        Wants      -12.50 + 260.00 =   247.50
        Mom reserve                  3,000.00

Step 4. You answer "move to savings" for needs. The 85.20 moves.
        Needs    475.20 - 85.20 =   390.00
        Savings  2,150.00 + 85.20 = 2,235.20

Step 5. How much should be in the savings account (rule 3.8)?
        Savings + mom reserve = 2,235.20 + 3,000.00 = 5,235.20
        The account holds 3,000.00, so you still need to move 2,235.20.

Check: mom reserve 3,000.00 | savings 2,235.20 | needs 390.00 | wants 247.50 | still to move 2,235.20

EXAMPLE 5. A week of spending (rules 4.1 to 4.8)
========================================================================

Your rules: anything with "GROCER" goes to needs, "CAFE" goes to wants.
Before: Needs 750.00 | Wants 500.00

Step 1. Dinner at MADE UP CAFE shows up pending for 40.00.
        It counts right away (4.1, 4.6). The CAFE rule picks wants.
        Wants  500.00 - 40.00 = 460.00

Step 2. The dinner posts at 48.00 (you added a tip).
        The posted charge replaces the pending one (4.6). Only the
        8.00 difference comes out.
        Wants  460.00 - 8.00 = 452.00

Step 3. 120.00 at MADE UP HARDWARE. No rule matches, so it is unsorted
        and waits in review (4.2, 4.3).
        Unsorted spending 120.00
        You split it: 100.00 to needs, 20.00 to wants (4.4).
        The parts add up to 120.00, so the split is accepted.
        Needs  750.00 - 100.00 = 650.00
        Wants  452.00 -  20.00 = 432.00
        Unsorted spending  0.00

Step 4. Lunch at MADE UP CAFE for 35.00, then refunded in full.
        Wants  432.00 - 35.00 = 397.00
        The refund goes back where the lunch came from (4.5).
        Wants  397.00 + 35.00 = 432.00

Step 5. A 29.00 late fee on the everyday card goes to review, with needs
        suggested (4.8). You pick needs.
        Needs  650.00 - 29.00 = 621.00

Check: needs 621.00 | wants 432.00 | unsorted spending 0.00

EXAMPLE 6. Card payments and the old card (rules 5.1 to 5.5, 3.8)
========================================================================

Before: Savings 1,000.00 | Needs 900.00 | Mom reserve 500.00
        Old card debt 3,000.00
        The savings account at the bank holds 900.00.

Step 1. You pay the everyday card 450.00 from checking.
        Not spending: the purchases were already counted (5.1).
        No bucket changes.

Step 2. You move 600.00 from checking to savings (5.3).
        No bucket changes. Only "still to move" changes (3.8):
        Should be in savings: 1,000.00 + 500.00 = 1,500.00
        Savings account now: 900.00 + 600.00   = 1,500.00
        Still to move: 1,500.00 - 1,500.00     =     0.00

Step 3. You pay the old card 200.00 from checking (5.2).
        It comes out of needs, on a debt payoff line.
        Needs  900.00 - 200.00     =   700.00
        Debt   3,000.00 - 200.00   = 2,800.00

Step 4. The old card charges 35.50 interest (5.5).
        Debt goes up. No bucket changes until you pay it.
        Debt   2,800.00 + 35.50    = 2,835.50

Step 5. A forgotten subscription charges 12.99 on the old card (5.4).
        It is flagged in review. Debt goes up, no bucket changes.
        Debt   2,835.50 + 12.99    = 2,848.49

Check: savings 1,000.00 | needs 700.00 | mom reserve 500.00 | old card debt 2,848.49 | still to move 0.00

EXAMPLE 7. Money in that is not a paycheck (rules 6.1, 3.2, 3.3)
========================================================================

Before: Mom reserve 2,800.00. Everything else 0.00.

Step 1. A 2,000.00 tax refund arrives. It is not a paycheck, so it waits
        in review as unsorted money in (6.1). You pick "income", so it is
        treated like a paycheck: reserve first, then the split.
        The reserve takes 1,500.00 off the top, but only 200.00 fits under
        the 3,000.00 cap (3,000.00 - 2,800.00).
        To mom reserve                            200.00
        Does not fit, goes to savings           1,300.00
        Rest to split (2,000.00 - 1,500.00)       500.00
        Savings 50% = 250.00 | Needs 30% = 150.00 | Wants 20% = 100.00
        Savings 1,300.00 + 250.00 = 1,550.00

Step 2. A friend pays you back 25.00 for lunch. You pick "payback" into
        wants.
        Wants  100.00 + 25.00 = 125.00

Step 3. Your roommate sends 60.00 for a utility bill you will pay for
        them. You pick "not mine", so it goes to held for others (7.1).
        Held for others 60.00

Nothing is left waiting in review.

Check: mom reserve 3,000.00 | savings 1,550.00 | needs 150.00 | wants 125.00 | held for others 60.00 | unsorted money in 0.00


========================================================================
EXAMPLE 8. Other people's money (rules 7.1 to 7.5)
========================================================================

Before: Wants 200.00 | Held for others 0.00

Step 1. Your roommate sends you 90.00 for their part of a bill. You mark
        it "not mine" (7.1). It is not income and is not split.
        Held for others  0.00 + 90.00 = 90.00

Step 2. You send that 90.00 on to the landlord, and mark it "not mine"
        (7.2). It is not spending.
        Held for others 90.00 - 90.00 = 0.00   (back to zero, 7.3)

Step 3. You pay 45.00 for a friend's concert ticket and sort it to wants.
        Wants  200.00 - 45.00 = 155.00

Step 4. The friend pays you back 45.00. A payback goes into the bucket you
        pick (7.5, 6.1). You pick wants.
        Wants  155.00 + 45.00 = 200.00

Step 5. You pay 30.00 for a neighbor's package and mark it "not mine".
        Held for others  0.00 - 30.00 = -30.00
        Negative means someone owes you (7.3): the neighbor owes 30.00.

Check: wants 200.00 | held for others -30.00 | unsorted spending 0.00
